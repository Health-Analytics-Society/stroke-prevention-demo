# Recommended Next Steps

This document maps 10 modifiable risk factors from the dataset to actionable prevention tips. Column names match the dataset exactly (see `docs/content/data_dictionary.md`).

---

**Risk factor:** `smoke`  
**Why it matters:** Smoking significantly increases the risk of blood clots and arterial damage, which are leading causes of stroke.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Speak with your doctor about a smoking cessation plan or nicotine replacement therapy.
- Explore resources such as the CDC's SmokefreeTXT program or a local quit line.
- Set a quit date and tell a trusted person to help you stay accountable.

---

**Risk factor:** `alcohol ` *(note: trailing space in dataset column name)*  
**Why it matters:** Heavy alcohol use raises blood pressure and is associated with an increased risk of stroke.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Track your weekly alcohol intake and compare it to recommended guidelines (≤1 drink/day for women, ≤2 for men).
- Talk to your doctor if you find it difficult to reduce or stop drinking.
- Consider alcohol-free days each week as a starting point for reducing consumption.

---

**Risk factor:** `sleep time`  
**Why it matters:** Both too little and too much sleep are associated with higher cardiovascular risk.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep per night as recommended by the American Academy of Sleep Medicine.
- Maintain a consistent sleep schedule, even on weekends.
- Discuss persistent sleep problems (falling asleep, staying asleep) with a healthcare provider.

---

**Risk factor:** `Body Mass Index`  
**Why it matters:** Higher BMI is associated with increased blood pressure, diabetes risk, and cardiovascular disease, all of which raise stroke risk.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Work with a doctor or dietitian to set a realistic, gradual weight loss goal if BMI is in the overweight or obese range.
- Incorporate at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity per week.
- Focus on sustainable dietary changes rather than short-term diets.

---

**Risk factor:** `Waist Circumference`  
**Why it matters:** Central obesity (high waist circumference) is a strong independent predictor of cardiovascular risk even when overall BMI appears normal.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Measure waist circumference regularly (healthy: <88 cm for women, <102 cm for men per WHO guidelines).
- Reduce intake of refined carbohydrates and added sugars, which contribute to visceral fat.
- Combine aerobic exercise with strength training to reduce abdominal fat.

---

**Risk factor:** `Systolic blood pressure`  
**Why it matters:** High systolic blood pressure (hypertension) is the single largest modifiable risk factor for stroke.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Monitor blood pressure regularly at home or at a pharmacy.
- Reduce sodium intake and follow a DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) eating pattern.
- Take any prescribed blood pressure medications consistently and discuss adjustments with your doctor.

---

**Risk factor:** `Diastolic blood pressure`  
**Why it matters:** Elevated diastolic pressure contributes to sustained arterial stress and raises stroke risk.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Follow the same lifestyle steps as for systolic pressure (diet, exercise, sodium reduction).
- Avoid caffeine and stimulants before blood pressure readings.
- Discuss medication options with your doctor if lifestyle changes are insufficient.

---

**Risk factor:** `Fasting Glucose`  
**Why it matters:** Elevated fasting glucose indicates pre-diabetes or diabetes, which doubles the risk of stroke.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Get fasting glucose checked at least annually if you have other risk factors.
- Reduce intake of sugary beverages and high-glycemic foods.
- Aim for regular physical activity, which improves insulin sensitivity.

---

**Risk factor:** `Dietary fiber`  
**Why it matters:** Higher dietary fiber intake is associated with lower blood pressure, better glucose control, and reduced cardiovascular risk.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Aim for at least 25 g/day (women) or 38 g/day (men) of dietary fiber per USDA guidelines.
- Increase intake of whole grains, legumes, fruits, and vegetables.
- Add fiber gradually to avoid gastrointestinal discomfort.

---

**Risk factor:** `Sodium`  
**Why it matters:** High sodium intake is a major driver of elevated blood pressure, which is the top risk factor for stroke.  
**Recommended next steps:**
- Aim for less than 2,300 mg of sodium per day (American Heart Association recommendation).
- Read nutrition labels and choose low-sodium alternatives when possible.
- Cook more meals at home to control the amount of added salt.
