# Recommended Next Steps

This page maps 10 modifiable risk factors from the dataset to plain-language actions. Column names match the dataset exactly.

---

## 1. Risk factor: `smoke`
**Why it matters:** Smoking damages blood vessels and significantly raises the risk of blood clots that can cause a stroke.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Talk to your doctor about a quit plan or nicotine replacement options.
- Explore free programs such as 1-800-QUIT-NOW or the Smokefree.gov app.
- Set a quit date and tell someone you trust to help hold you accountable.

---

## 2. Risk factor: `alcohol ` *(note: column name includes a trailing space)*
**Why it matters:** Heavy or frequent alcohol use raises blood pressure and can trigger irregular heart rhythms, both of which increase stroke risk.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Aim for no more than 1 drink per day for women or 2 for men, per general public health guidance.
- Track your weekly intake for two weeks to see your baseline before making changes.
- If cutting back feels difficult, speak with a healthcare provider about support resources.

---

## 3. Risk factor: `sleep time`
**Why it matters:** Both too little and too much sleep are linked to higher rates of high blood pressure and inflammation, which raise stroke risk.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep per night for most adults.
- Keep a consistent bedtime and wake time, even on weekends.
- If you consistently sleep fewer than 6 or more than 10 hours, mention it to your doctor.

---

## 4. Risk factor: `Minutes sedentary activity`
**Why it matters:** Long stretches of sitting slow circulation and contribute to weight gain, high blood pressure, and poor blood sugar control.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Break up sitting time with a short walk or stretch every 30–60 minutes.
- Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate movement per week.
- Use a phone alarm or fitness tracker to remind you to move throughout the day.

---

## 5. Risk factor: `Body Mass Index`
**Why it matters:** Higher BMI is associated with increased blood pressure, blood sugar problems, and inflammation — all stroke risk factors.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Work with your doctor to set a realistic weight goal rather than focusing on a number alone.
- Small changes like reducing portion sizes and adding daily walks can meaningfully lower BMI over time.
- Avoid crash diets; gradual, steady change is more sustainable and safer.

---

## 6. Risk factor: `Systolic blood pressure`
**Why it matters:** High systolic pressure (the top number) puts extra strain on artery walls and is one of the strongest predictors of stroke.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Monitor your blood pressure regularly at home or at a pharmacy kiosk.
- Reduce sodium intake and increase potassium-rich foods like bananas, beans, and leafy greens.
- Follow your doctor's guidance if you have been prescribed blood pressure medication — do not stop it without consulting them.

---

## 7. Risk factor: `Fasting Glucose`
**Why it matters:** Elevated fasting blood sugar signals insulin resistance or diabetes, both of which damage blood vessels over time.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Get a fasting glucose test at your next checkup if you have not had one recently.
- Reduce added sugars and refined carbohydrates in your diet.
- Regular physical activity — even 20–30 minutes of walking most days — can improve fasting glucose.

---

## 8. Risk factor: `Glycohemoglobin`
**Why it matters:** HbA1c reflects your average blood sugar over the past 2–3 months; a high level means cells and blood vessels have been exposed to excess sugar for an extended period.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Ask your doctor for an HbA1c test if you are at risk for diabetes or haven't been tested in the last year.
- Focus on consistent dietary changes — daily choices matter more than occasional ones for HbA1c.
- If your HbA1c is above 5.7%, talk to your doctor about a prediabetes prevention program.

---

## 9. Risk factor: `High-density lipoprotein`
**Why it matters:** HDL is the "good" cholesterol that helps clear harmful fats from arteries; low HDL is linked to greater cardiovascular and stroke risk.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Regular aerobic exercise (brisk walking, cycling, swimming) is one of the most effective ways to raise HDL.
- Replace trans fats and refined carbohydrates with healthy fats like olive oil, nuts, and avocado.
- If you smoke, quitting is one of the fastest ways to improve HDL levels.

---

## 10. Risk factor: `Dietary fiber`
**Why it matters:** Low fiber intake is linked to higher cholesterol, blood sugar spikes, and weight gain — all of which contribute to stroke risk over time.

**Recommended next steps:**
- Aim for 25–38 grams of fiber per day from whole foods (beans, vegetables, fruits, whole grains).
- Swap refined grains for whole-grain versions of bread, pasta, and rice.
- Add a serving of vegetables or legumes to at least two meals per day as a simple starting goal.
